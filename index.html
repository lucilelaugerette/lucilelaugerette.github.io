When Words Shape Action: Editorial Guidelines, Media Narratives and Audience Reactions Job Market Paper

Draft available upon request

Request draft
Abstract
How the press covers femicide matters. Between 2018 and 2025, 43 French local newspapers adopted editorial guidelines on reporting gender-based violence, requiring legally precise language and discouraging euphemisms. Combining exhaustive femicide records with article-level text analysis, I show that adoption raises the probability that an outlet covers a local femicide by 87% and the use of recommended legal terminology by 132%. These shifts move audiences: departments more exposed to guideline-compliant coverage record more signatures on petitions against gender-based violence and, suggestively, more calls to the national 3919 helpline. A low-cost editorial rule can turn gender-based violence into a more salient public issue.
